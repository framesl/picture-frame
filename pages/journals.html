A Handmade Web by J. R. Carpenter

Creating something by hand has always made me feel better about myself than making it with a tool of some kind. Obviously if it’s something I literally can’t do without the tool then I’d prefer to make it easier for myself by using it. However, in a world filled with AI, having to clarify that I don’t mean AI in the sentence above, but LITERAL tools is a bit disheartening. In this day and age, making something by hand is seen as a mockery to some, and an achievement to others. I simply don’t want to use AI because I know I’m better than it. I haven’t used it my entire life and I’ve gotten this far, so clearly I don’t need it to help me now. That being said, creating something by hand digitally gives me the same amount of satisfaction as making something by hand physically. Mostly because I almost always make something digital, and then print it to turn it into a physical object. Therefore, creating my own webpage is something entirely new/different than my previous projects, so every single time I try something new and it works: I get so excited! I made it with the knowledge given to me combined with my will to achieve what I want! It’s mine and no one can take that away from me!

A Rant on Technology by Ursula K. Le Guin

I think I’ve read this before for another class, but it’s always nice to read something like this again as a reminder of what technology is. It’s not just our laptops, cars, tvs, etc. It’s our chairs, paper, ink, clothes, anything that was once nothing until humanity got their hands on its ingredients and decided to create. Technology isn’t always the high-tech stuff people think it is today, and that actually reminded me of when I tried to get a minor in Computer Science. Long story short, it didn’t work out, but it wasn’t because I wasn’t trying (I was actively putting in so much effort it was insane), it was because I lacked certain information that the people around me refused to tell me. Specifically my teacher, who upon informing him that I had no idea what he was talking about, would just sit and stare at me and tell me to guess. In a room full of people who knew how to do so much with what we were learning about, I felt inadequate, and with the lack of teaching, withdrew to stick to what I know: Design. Thinking back on that though, a lot of my classmates were genuinely impressed when I told them about the things I had to make in my Graphic Design classes that they wouldn’t even have a clue on how to start. Everyone learns how to do certain things, and something that may be so difficult or easy to them could be completely different for another person, and that’s okay! Technology is what comes out of people working together, and I think that’s something that’s being forgotten.

My Website is a Shifting House… by Laurel Schwulst

I really enjoyed the part of the essay where she said “The web is what we make it,” because that’s really the best way to describe it. Without any metaphors, analogies, etc. the web is what we choose to do with it. It could be anything, and is anything depending on where you look, but it’s not everything. I think it’s always important to have a place to store your interests, I mean I have enough lists, pictures, drawings, and shelves storing things I enjoy to do I don’t even know what with. However, having so many places storing many things can sometimes make it so you lose stuff. If you make a website, you could store everything there. I’m not saying you should do that for everything (it is important as a portfolio), but websites can contain memories that are no longer accessible in real life. You can’t always keep everything, so storing it digitally on something you made always feels more rewarding whenever you reminisce. Overall, the web is an overarching storage of memories from people all over, and will continue to be until it inevitably ends.
